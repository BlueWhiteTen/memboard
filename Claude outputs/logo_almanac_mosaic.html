Follow-up experiment

Almanac × Mosaic

Four ways of fusing the two: a dated record (almanac) as the container, made of unevenly-sized pieces (mosaic) instead of a grid of identical squares — which is what read too close to the Windows logo last time.

A — TORN PAGE, UNEVEN TILES
A calendar page, cut into four unequal pieces
Keeps the almanac's hanging-tabs shape, but the inside is split off-centre into four different-sized tiles instead of a symmetric grid — closer to a scrapbook page than a spreadsheet.
B — STACK OF RECORDS
A stack of dated pages, front one tiled
Three layered pages read as an ongoing record kept over time; only the top one shows the mosaic split, as if each page collects its own set of pieces.
C — STAMPED SEAL, UNEVEN WEDGES
A record stamped as a seal, cut in three
Trades the page shape for a stamped medallion — feels more like an official, dated entry — divided into three uneven wedges rather than a neat pie chart.
D — MONTH GRID, ONE MARKED DAY
A calendar with a few cells filled in, unevenly
Reads most literally as "a calendar of memories" — a loose scatter of filled-in, differently-sized cells rather than a full even grid, with one cell solid to mark "today's entry."
Pick a direction (or none of them) and I'll refine it — sharpen the proportions, try it small at favicon size, pair it with a name.
